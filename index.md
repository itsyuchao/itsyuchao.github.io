## Bio

- In my second year as a **PhD student in Biomedical Engineering** at [Duke](https://bme.duke.edu/). My advisors are Dr. Nanthia Suthana and Dr. Greg Cogan.  
- I trained clinically as a **Doctor of Physical Therapy** at [MGH Institute](https://www.mghihp.edu). While there, I was a research assistant/fellow working with Dr. Teresa Kimberley on various TMS and fMRI projects on focal dystonia ([link](https://movementdisorders.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/mds.70021)) and healthy adults ([link](https://www.brainstimjrnl.com/article/S1935-861X(23)01960-5/fulltext)).
- Previously, I worked as a research specialist at [Penn Medicine](https://www.med.upenn.edu/lcns/) studying aphasia recovery and TMS in stroke and Alzheimer's disease. My supervisors were Dr. Branch Coslett and Dr. Roy Hamilton.  
- Before that, I graduated *summa cum laude* with a **B.A. in Cognitive Science** from [Haverford College](https://www.haverford.edu/). This was where my science journey started.
  - My undergraduate thesis explored the neural correlates of beauty in literary stories ([link](https://www.nature.com/articles/s42003-021-02926-0)). I was advised by Dr. Franziska Hartung and Dr. Anjan Chatterjee at [Penn Center for Neuroaesthetics](https://neuroaesthetics.med.upenn.edu/).
  - My summer research investigated how beauty in peacock feathers may be a form of camouflage ([link](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article/authors?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0210924)). I was advised by Dr. Suzanne Amador Kane in [Haverford Biophysics Lab](https://www.haverford.edu/users/samador).
